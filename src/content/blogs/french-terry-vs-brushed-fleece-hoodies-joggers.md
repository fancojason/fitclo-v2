# French Terry vs Brushed Fleece: Which Fabric Is Better for Hoodies, Joggers and Sweatshirts?

Two hoodies may look almost identical on the outside but feel completely different when you put them on. The reason is often the fabric's inner surface: one may use **French terry (loopback fabric)**, while the other uses **brushed-back fleece**.

For activewear and casualwear brands, that choice affects the garment's warmth, comfort, silhouette, seasonality and sourcing specifications. This guide explains the differences and how to choose the right fabric for hoodies, sweatshirts, joggers, shorts and matching lounge sets.

## Quick Answer: What Is the Difference Between French Terry and Brushed Fleece?

**French terry has visible, unbrushed yarn loops on the inside and usually a relatively smooth outer face. Brushed fleece has a raised, fuzzy inner surface created by brushing or napping.** As a result, French terry often suits breathable, versatile layers, while brushed fleece is generally preferred when softness and added warmth are priorities.

These are descriptions of **fabric construction and finishing**, not fiber composition. Either can be produced in cotton or other approved fiber blends. The exact performance depends on the fabric's GSM, yarns, structure and finishing, so always assess a physical sample before confirming bulk production.

## 1. What Is French Terry Fabric?

French terry, also known as **loopback fabric** or **unbrushed sweatshirt fabric**, is a knitted textile with small loops or floats visible on the reverse side. The face is typically smoother, making it suitable for garments with a clean, understated look.

![Close-up of gray French terry fabric showing its knitted outer surface and visible loopback yarns on the underside](/images/blogs/french-terry-vs-brushed-fleece-hoodies-joggers/french-terry-loopback-original.webp)

*French Terry / Loopback Interior: the inner loops remain visible rather than being brushed into a fuzzy surface. Reader-supplied reference image; it does not establish a specific Fitclo stocked material.*

### Key characteristics of French terry

- **Distinctive loopback interior:** the reverse side shows small knitted loops.
- **Comfortable layering:** often a practical option for indoor wear, transitional weather and casual movement.
- **Moderate warmth:** can provide substance without the plush inner pile of brushed fleece.
- **Versatile drape:** available in lighter and heavier versions depending on yarn and GSM.
- **Many compositions:** available as 100% cotton or blended constructions; confirm the exact composition on the fabric specification.

### Which garments work well in French terry?

French terry is a strong starting point for **lightweight and midweight hoodies, crewneck sweatshirts, relaxed joggers, casual shorts, oversized pullovers and matching athleisure sets**. It can also suit warm-up pieces designed for layering before or after exercise.

For a spring/autumn collection or an indoor-to-outdoor lifestyle line, a well-selected French terry can provide a balanced combination of structure, comfort and versatility.

### What does “100% French Terry Cotton” mean?

A buyer asking for **100% cotton French terry** is specifying two separate things:

1. **Fiber content:** 100% cotton.
2. **Fabric structure:** French terry with an unbrushed loopback interior.

It does **not** automatically mean the fabric is thick, pre-shrunk, stretch-recovery optimized or brushed inside. Those requirements need to be confirmed separately. A 100% cotton French terry garment may have natural knit stretch, but it should not be assumed to perform like a nylon/spandex compression legging.

## 2. What Is Brushed Fleece Fabric?

In this article, **brushed fleece** means **brushed-back sweatshirt fleece**, not polar fleece or sherpa fleece. Its inner yarns are brushed or napped to create a soft, raised surface. This plush interior can trap more air and commonly gives the garment a cozier, warmer feel.

![Close-up of red brushed fleece showing its knit front side and soft, raised brushed interior with English labels](/images/blogs/french-terry-vs-brushed-fleece-hoodies-joggers/brushed-fleece-inside-original.webp)

*Brushed Fleece / Soft Napped Interior: the reverse side has a raised, fuzzy finish. Reader-supplied reference image; it does not establish a specific Fitclo stocked material.*

### Key characteristics of brushed fleece

- **Soft inner surface:** brushed fibers create a fluffy or velvety hand feel.
- **Greater warmth potential:** the raised surface can add insulating loft.
- **Cold-weather appeal:** well suited to cozy, winter-focused collections.
- **Flexible composition:** may be made from 100% cotton, cotton/polyester blends or synthetic fibers; never infer composition from the word “fleece.”
- **Finish requires testing:** pilling, surface shedding, shrinkage and appearance after laundering depend on the fabric and process.

### Which garments work well in brushed fleece?

Brushed fleece is commonly selected for **winter hoodies, heavyweight crewnecks, cozy sweatpants, relaxed tracksuits, fleece-lined joggers, lounge sets and warm-up garments** where warmth and a soft inner feel are important.

For brands positioning a collection around autumn/winter comfort, brushed fleece may be the better choice than an unbrushed loopback knit of otherwise comparable construction.

## 3. French Terry vs Brushed Fleece: Comparison Table

| Feature | French Terry (Loopback) | Brushed-Back Fleece |
|---|---|---|
| Inner surface | Visible unbrushed loops | Raised, brushed, fuzzy surface |
| Outer appearance | Usually relatively smooth | Often relatively smooth |
| Typical hand feel | Textured inside; less plush | Softer, loftier inside |
| Warmth | Usually more moderate | Usually warmer when comparable in other specifications |
| Breathability in use | Often a good option for versatile layers | May feel warmer, depending on GSM and composition |
| Best seasons | Transitional seasons and year-round layering | Autumn/winter and colder conditions |
| Popular garments | Hoodies, sweatshirts, shorts, joggers, lounge sets | Warm hoodies, sweatpants, winter tracksuits, cozy sets |
| Fiber composition | Cotton or blends | Cotton, cotton/polyester or other blends |
| Main sample checks | Loopback quality, shrinkage, drape, recovery | Brushing quality, pilling, shedding, shrinkage |

**Important:** These comparisons describe common tendencies—not guaranteed test results. A heavy French terry can be warmer than a lightweight fleece, and material performance must be verified on the selected sample.

## 4. Does GSM Determine Which Fabric Is Better?

**GSM means grams per square meter.** It is a measure of fabric mass per unit area, not a direct score for warmth, quality or durability. Two fabrics at 320 GSM can behave differently because one is loopback and the other is brushed.

For early discussions with a manufacturer, these broad ranges can help describe a design direction:

| Development direction | Illustrative GSM starting point | Suitable design goal |
|---|---:|---|
| Lighter French terry | Around 240–300 GSM | Easy-layering sweatshirts, relaxed shorts |
| Mid/heavier French terry | Around 300–400 GSM | Structured hoodies, matching jogger sets |
| Mid/heavier brushed fleece | Around 280–400+ GSM | Cozy sweatshirts, winter hoodies, sweatpants |

These are **illustrative sourcing ranges, not Fitclo's confirmed stocked specifications, fixed standards or performance guarantees**. Fabric weights overlap substantially; construction, shrinkage and finished garment measurements matter just as much. For a deeper explanation, read our [fabric GSM guide](https://www.fitcloo.com/blogs/fabric-weight-selection-guide/).

## 5. Which Fabric Should Your Clothing Brand Choose?

### Choose French terry when you want:

- A comfortable **three-season hoodie** rather than a heavy winter piece.
- **Oversized sweatshirts** with a cleaner, less fluffy interior.
- **Casual shorts and joggers** for warmer climates or everyday layering.
- An identifiable **100% cotton loopback** story, if the selected fabric's fiber content is independently confirmed.

### Choose brushed fleece when you want:

- A **winter hoodie or warm tracksuit** with a softer inner touch.
- **Lounge sets** marketed primarily for coziness and comfort.
- **Post-workout warm-up layers** that feel insulating in cooler conditions.
- A more plush interior, subject to approved fabric and wash testing.

### Think about the end use—not only the fabric name

For a high-sweat gym legging or fitted sports bra, these sweatshirt fabrics are generally not direct substitutes for an engineered stretch fabric. A performance product may require a different combination of stretch, recovery, support, opacity and moisture handling. ## 6. How to Specify French Terry or Brushed Fleece to a Manufacturer

If you are preparing an OEM/ODM brief, avoid sending only “French terry, good quality.” A more complete fabric request helps the development team quote and sample the right material.

**Example A — French terry hoodie:**

> Fabric: 100% cotton French terry (unbrushed loopback). Target approximately 320 GSM. Smooth face with visible interior loops. Please confirm finished fabric width, color options, shrinkage performance and swatch availability before sampling.

**Example B — Brushed fleece tracksuit:**

> Fabric: brushed-back sweatshirt fleece. Target approximately 330–360 GSM. Soft napped interior, smooth outer face. Fiber composition to be confirmed before quotation. Please provide a fabric swatch and confirm pilling, shrinkage and wash appearance.

The descriptions above are **buyer brief examples**, not specifications of a particular Fitclo inventory item.

### Quality-control checks before bulk production

1. **Confirm composition:** a “cotton fleece” quote is not automatically 100% cotton; request the full fiber percentages.
2. **Confirm GSM and width:** use the finished fabric specification, not an approximate visual judgment.
3. **Inspect both sides:** check loop regularity or brushing uniformity, depending on the chosen structure.
4. **Wash-test the sample:** evaluate shrinkage, twisting, colorfastness, surface appearance, pilling and shedding where relevant.
5. **Approve fit on the actual fabric:** drape and dimensional changes can affect hoodies, waistbands, cuffs and joggers.
6. **Test logos and trims:** review embroidery, heat transfer, prints, labels and their behavior after washing.
7. **Lock the production reference:** approve color, fabric hand feel, finished measurements and decoration placement together.

## 7. Frequently Asked Questions

### Is French terry the same as fleece?

They are closely related knit constructions, but in common sweatshirt terminology **French terry keeps the reverse loops unbrushed**, while **brushed fleece has a napped, fuzzy reverse surface**. Some suppliers use “fleece” broadly, so ask for photographs of both fabric sides and the exact specification.

### Is French terry always 100% cotton?

No. French terry describes a structure, not a mandatory fiber composition. It can be 100% cotton or a blend. The fiber percentages should be stated separately.

### Is brushed fleece always polyester?

No. Brushed sweatshirt fleece can be cotton, cotton/polyester or other fiber mixes. Do not confuse brushed sweatshirt fleece with polyester polar fleece.

### Which is better for hoodies: French terry or fleece?

For adaptable, lighter-feeling layers, French terry is often a good choice. For a plush winter hoodie, brushed fleece may fit the brief better. Compare samples of similar GSM and composition before making the final decision.

### Which fabric is better for joggers and sweatpants?

Both can work. French terry is a practical choice for everyday or transitional-season joggers, while brushed fleece is commonly used for warmer lounge and winter sweatpants. Confirm stretch and recovery, waistband construction, shrinkage and fit during sampling.

### Does higher GSM always mean better quality?

No. GSM measures fabric weight. Fiber quality, knitting consistency, finishing, pilling resistance, shrinkage control and garment workmanship all influence product quality.
