# From Tech Pack to Final Sample: Complete Activewear Development Process

Turning an activewear idea into a production-ready garment is not a single sampling step. It is a controlled development process that connects design intent, fabric performance, pattern engineering, garment construction, branding, fit testing, revisions, and final approval.

For brands working with a **custom activewear manufacturer**, the goal is not simply to receive a sample that looks similar to a sketch. The goal is to create a repeatable product standard that can move into bulk production with fewer misunderstandings, fewer avoidable revisions, and clearer quality expectations.

Fitclo supports activewear brands through this OEM/ODM development journey, from initial Tech Pack review to final sample approval.

## Quick Answer: How Does Custom Activewear Development Work?

A typical **yoga wear development process** follows this sequence:

**Tech Pack Review → Requirement Confirmation → Fabric Selection → Pattern Development → Prototype Sample → Sample Review → Revision → Final Sample Approval → Bulk Production Preparation → Quality Control**

The Tech Pack defines the product requirements. Fabric selection determines performance and hand feel. Pattern making converts the design into a wearable shape. The prototype sample verifies fit, construction, appearance, and branding. Revisions continue until the buyer approves a final sample that can serve as the reference for bulk production.

For standard activewear developments, Fitclo's normal sample development time is typically **7–10 working days** after the required development details and materials are confirmed. More complex designs may require additional time.

## 1. Tech Pack Review: Turning a Design into Production Instructions

The Tech Pack is the foundation of custom activewear development. A good Tech Pack allows the manufacturer to understand not only what the garment should look like, but also how it should fit, stretch, support the body, and be constructed.

![Tech pack review for custom activewear development](/images/blog/activewear-development-process/01-activewear-tech-pack-review.webp "Tech pack review for custom activewear development")

At the beginning of a project, we review information such as:

- Technical drawings or flat sketches
- Measurement specifications
- Size chart and grading information
- Fabric composition or performance requirements
- GSM requirements, if specified
- Color references or Pantone references
- Seam and construction details
- Waistband construction
- Strap, neckline, cup, or support details
- Logo size and placement
- Heat-transfer, silicone, embroidery, or other branding requirements
- Main labels and care labels
- Hangtags
- Packaging requirements

A Tech Pack does not need to be perfect before a development discussion begins. However, every unclear point should be confirmed before sampling whenever possible.

### What does the manufacturer check during Tech Pack review?

A professional review should answer questions such as:

- Can the requested construction be manufactured reliably?
- Is the chosen fabric suitable for the intended fit?
- Are the measurements internally consistent?
- Is the logo method appropriate for the fabric and placement?
- Could a seam, waistband, strap, or panel create comfort or stretch problems?
- Are any details missing that could lead to different interpretations during sampling?

The output of this stage should be a clear, shared understanding of the product before fabric is cut.

## 2. Design and Requirement Confirmation

After the Tech Pack review, the buyer and manufacturer align on any open points.

This stage is important because a drawing can show the shape of a garment while still leaving production questions unanswered.

For example, a leggings design may still require confirmation of:

- High-rise or mid-rise waistband
- Front seam or no-front-seam construction
- Compression level
- Waistband height
- Pocket structure
- Gusset construction
- Seam placement
- Hem finish
- Logo position
- Matte or brushed surface preference

For a sports bra, the development discussion may include:

- Light, medium, or higher support
- Removable cups or no cups
- Lining structure
- Strap width
- Elastic support
- Neckline depth
- Back construction

The purpose is to convert design language into production language.

## 3. Fabric Selection and Material Confirmation

Fabric selection has a major influence on the final performance of activewear. Two garments with the same pattern can fit and perform very differently when made from different fabrics.

![Fabric selection process for private label activewear](/images/blog/activewear-development-process/02-activewear-fabric-selection.webp "Fabric selection process for private label activewear")

Common nylon/spandex fabric compositions used in activewear development include:

- **75% Nylon + 25% Spandex**
- **78% Nylon + 22% Spandex**
- **68% Nylon + 32% Spandex**

The best option depends on the design, target hand feel, compression, weight, stretch, and intended use.

### Key fabric characteristics to evaluate

A **private label activewear** project should evaluate more than composition alone.

| Fabric Factor | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Softness | Influences comfort and premium hand feel |
| Stretch | Determines movement range |
| Recovery | Helps the garment return to shape after stretching |
| Compression | Affects support and body-hugging feel |
| Opacity | Important for squat-proof performance |
| Breathability | Influences comfort during training |
| GSM | Affects thickness, support, drape, and coverage |
| Surface finish | Creates matte, smooth, brushed, or other visual/hand-feel effects |
| Color performance | Affects consistency between sample and bulk |
| Pilling resistance | Important for repeated wear and friction areas |

### Why fabric should be confirmed before finalizing fit

Fabric elasticity changes the way a pattern behaves on the body. If a sample is approved in one fabric and bulk production is switched to a significantly different fabric, the fit may change.

For this reason, fabric selection and pattern development should be treated as connected decisions rather than separate tasks.

## 4. Pattern Making: Converting Specifications into Fit

Pattern development is where the two-dimensional design becomes a three-dimensional garment.

![Pattern making process in activewear product development](/images/blog/activewear-development-process/03-activewear-pattern-making.webp "Pattern making process in activewear product development")

The technical team develops the pattern based on:

- Tech Pack measurements
- Fabric stretch and recovery
- Intended compression
- Garment silhouette
- Seam construction
- Size and fit expectations
- Waistband structure
- Bust support
- Leg opening or hem requirements
- Design panels and style lines

### Activewear pattern making is different from ordinary fashion pattern making

Activewear often uses highly elastic fabrics, so the pattern must account for stretch, recovery, movement, and negative ease.

The pattern maker must balance:

**Fit + Comfort + Support + Mobility + Appearance**

For example, overly aggressive reduction can create excessive compression or transparency, while insufficient reduction can cause loose areas, waistband movement, or poor recovery.

The first pattern is therefore a technical hypothesis that must be verified through the physical sample.

## 5. Prototype Sample Production

Once the fabric direction and first pattern are confirmed, the sample room produces the prototype.

![Activewear sample room for prototype development](/images/blog/activewear-development-process/04-activewear-sample-room.webp "Activewear sample room for prototype development")

The basic workflow includes:

1. Fabric preparation
2. Pattern checking
3. Cutting
4. Sewing
5. Construction finishing
6. Logo application, when included in the sample
7. Label application, when required
8. Garment finishing
9. Initial quality inspection

For standard developments, the normal sample development time is typically **7–10 working days** after the necessary specifications and materials are confirmed.

Complex constructions, special fabrics, custom trims, unusual branding techniques, or multiple development changes may require more time.

### What is the purpose of the first sample?

The first prototype is not simply a sales sample. It is a development tool.

It helps verify whether the specifications work in a real garment and reveals issues that may not be visible on a drawing.

Common questions include:

- Does the waistband stay in position?
- Is the rise correct?
- Is the compression appropriate?
- Are the seams comfortable?
- Does the bra provide the intended support?
- Does the fabric become transparent under stretch?
- Is the logo correctly positioned?
- Does the garment match the intended visual proportions?

## 6. Sample Review: What Should a Brand Check?

When the first sample arrives, the most useful feedback is structured feedback.

Instead of only saying "make it tighter" or "the shape is not right," brands should identify the exact area, measurement, and desired change whenever possible.

### Fit

Review:

- Overall size
- Waist fit
- Hip fit
- Rise
- Inseam
- Leg opening
- Bust fit
- Underband fit
- Strap position
- Armhole
- Neckline
- Length

### Movement and performance

Test the sample in realistic movement conditions:

- Squatting
- Stretching
- Walking
- Sitting
- Bending
- Raising the arms
- Light training movements

Check whether the garment shifts, rolls, digs into the body, restricts movement, or becomes transparent.

### Appearance

Check:

- Overall silhouette
- Panel proportions
- Color
- Surface finish
- Stitching appearance
- Logo size
- Logo placement
- Label position

### Workmanship

Inspect:

- Stitch consistency
- Seam alignment
- Loose threads
- Stretch seam performance
- Fabric damage
- Symmetry
- Edge finishing
- Branding application

## 7. How to Give Sample Feedback That Reduces Revision Time

Clear feedback helps both the brand and the **OEM activewear manufacturer in China** work more efficiently.

A useful feedback format is:

**Area → Current issue → Requested change → Reference measurement/photo**

Example:

> Waistband: sample feels 1.5 cm too high at center front. Please reduce center-front waistband height while keeping the back height unchanged.

This is more actionable than:

> Please make the waistband better.

For multiple changes, mark photos or technical drawings and send one consolidated revision list whenever possible.

### Good revision feedback should separate:

- Measurement changes
- Pattern changes
- Fabric changes
- Construction changes
- Color changes
- Logo changes
- Label or packaging changes

This reduces the chance that one adjustment is overlooked.

## 8. Sample Revision and Final Approval

After feedback is confirmed, the development team adjusts the required elements.

Typical revisions may involve:

- Pattern shape
- Measurements
- Waistband height
- Seam position
- Strap dimensions
- Leg length
- Fabric choice
- Construction method
- Logo size or position
- Label placement

Not every project requires the same number of sample rounds.

A relatively simple design with a complete Tech Pack and an existing proven fabric may reach approval quickly. A highly customized style may require additional revisions.

### What is a final approved sample?

The final approved sample is the version that both sides agree represents the intended product.

Before bulk production, the approved standard should align with the final:

- Measurements
- Pattern
- Fabric
- Color
- Construction
- Logo method
- Logo placement
- Labels
- Packaging requirements

Bulk production should begin only after these key specifications are confirmed.

## How to Reduce Activewear Sample Development Problems

Brands can make the development process more efficient by preparing five things before sampling begins:

### 1. Define the target customer

A compression training legging, a soft yoga legging, and a travel/lifestyle legging should not be developed with identical performance expectations.

### 2. Define the benchmark

If you want a particular level of softness, compression, waistband support, or fabric finish, provide a physical reference, fabric reference, or clear performance description.

### 3. Confirm the fabric direction early

Do not finalize fit in one fabric and then change to a completely different stretch structure at the end of development.

### 4. Consolidate feedback

Send one clear revision list instead of multiple fragmented messages from different team members.

### 5. Approve the exact production standard

Before bulk production, make sure both sides are referring to the same approved sample, Tech Pack version, material, color, and branding details.

## Ready-Stock Private Label vs. Full OEM Development

Not every activewear brand needs full OEM development for every product.

### Ready-stock private label

This approach is suitable when a brand wants to select existing styles and add branding elements such as:

- Logo
- Main label
- Care label
- Hangtag
- Packaging

It usually requires less product-development work because the garment design and pattern already exist.

### OEM/ODM custom development

This approach is appropriate when the brand wants to control:

- Original design
- Measurements
- Pattern
- Fabric
- Construction
- Color
- Branding
- Product details

For a new custom collection, the Tech Pack-to-final-sample process described in this guide is the more relevant workflow.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does it take to develop an activewear sample?

For standard custom developments, Fitclo's normal sample development time is typically **7–10 working days** after the required specifications and materials are confirmed. Complex designs may require additional time.

### Do I need a Tech Pack to develop custom activewear?

A complete Tech Pack is the clearest starting point, but brands can also begin with design drawings, reference images, measurements, and product requirements. Missing technical details should be confirmed before sampling.

### What fabrics are commonly used for custom yoga wear?

Common nylon/spandex options include **75% Nylon + 25% Spandex, 78% Nylon + 22% Spandex, and 68% Nylon + 32% Spandex**. The appropriate fabric depends on the desired softness, compression, stretch, recovery, GSM, opacity, and surface finish.

### Why is fabric selection important before pattern approval?

Different fabrics stretch and recover differently. Changing fabric after fit approval can change compression, measurements, drape, opacity, and overall fit.

### What should I check when I receive the first sample?

Check fit, measurements, movement, opacity, fabric hand feel, seam construction, stitching, shape, color, logo placement, labels, and other design details.

### Can the sample be revised before bulk production?

Yes. Pattern, measurements, construction, fabric details, and branding elements can be revised based on confirmed feedback. Bulk production should begin only after final sample approval.

### Can I customize the logo, labels, and packaging?

Private label and OEM/ODM projects can include logo application, labels, hangtags, and packaging based on the confirmed project requirements and selected production methods.

### What is the difference between a prototype sample and a final sample?

The prototype is used to test the first development version. The final sample is the approved version that represents the agreed product standard before bulk production.

## A Practical Development Principle

The most successful activewear development projects usually follow one simple rule:

**Confirm decisions early, test the garment physically, document every revision, and lock the final standard before bulk production.**

A clear process reduces ambiguity between the brand, pattern team, sample room, production team, and quality-control team.

For brands looking for a **custom activewear manufacturer** or **private label activewear** partner, the value of the supplier is not only the ability to sew a garment. It is the ability to translate product requirements into a repeatable manufacturing standard.
